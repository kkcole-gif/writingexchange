02

Labor Advocacy Playbook

Turning the CCCC Statement of Professional Guidance for New Faculty Members into adaptable institutional action.

The Playbook gives WPAs at every level of institutional power a structured process, from course leads with informal influence to tenured faculty with administrative authority. Each step adapts to institution type, position, and risk tolerance.

The resource addresses teaching load, course caps, teaching modality, contract transparency, performance evaluation, curriculum autonomy, and labor equity. It draws on professional statements issued by CCCC, CWPA, TYCA, and MLA.

CCCC statements are cited by title and date of adoption. Archived copies are held in the CCCC-L position statement repository (opens in a new tab). See the note on CCCC.

Key Professional Issues Addressed

  • Teaching load & course caps
  • Contract transparency & salary
  • DEI-informed curriculum policy
  • Faculty reappointment & evaluation
  • Teaching modality & scheduling
  • Curricular autonomy & shared governance

Process Overview

  1. Step 01

    Situational Self-Assessment

    Assess your institutional power, position type, and years in the role to understand your standing and risk tolerance before acting.

  2. Step 02

    Identify & Connect

    Map who else is affected, which spaces already exist for coalition-building, and how decisions on the issue are made at your institution.

  3. Step 03

    Gather Local Data

    Collect quantitative and qualitative data in coordination with institutional effectiveness, the registrar, and faculty. Document existing policies.

  4. Step 04

    Compare to National Standards

    Locate the relevant CCCC, CWPA, TYCA, MLA, and AAUP statements. Use national data to frame local findings with disciplinary authority.

  5. Step 05

    Plan a Sharing Event

    Decide the format, the audience, and whether to include leadership. Share findings to build a coalition and momentum for change.

  6. Step 06

    Move into Governance or Beyond

    If momentum exists, move through official channels. If not, consider the Trojan Horse approach described in the full Playbook, or go to the faculty senate, the union, the press, or an accreditor.

03

Data-Driven Advocacy

WPAs frame a program's value through data aligned with administrative priorities, while attending to equity, disciplinary values, and the ethics of data collection. These heuristics support data-driven advocacy from planning through circulation.

Heuristic 01: Planning Your Advocacy

Questions to Ask Before You Start

  • What is happening, and what is the exigence for this advocacy?
  • What is your goal, and why? Which genre does your audience expect?
  • Who is your audience, and which data is legible to them?
  • Where did this data come from? Which biases should you consider?
  • Who is available to help with data collection and analysis?
  • Which scholarship and national standards support your case?
  • Are you making a comparison? Which benchmark would be useful?
  • What are the limitations of your data for interpretation?
  • Which terms are legible to your audience (enrollment, retention, SCH)?
  • What is your plan for circulation, and why that method?
Heuristic 02: Class Size Case Study

Visualizing Class Size Data for Different Stakeholders

  • For campus leaders: a dashboard showing average class size by department, overlaid with cost and student credit hour (SCH) data
  • For faculty governance: class size distributions by course type, with student learning outcomes alongside cap size
  • For public advocacy: a comparison bar chart or scatter plot showing class size before and after cap increases, with retention or satisfaction rates
  • Equity check: are low-enrolling sections (internships, independent studies) distorting averages?
  • Transparency: does your visualization note its data sources and limitations?
  • Framing: present class size as a factor in retention, pedagogical quality, and student outcomes, as well as an efficiency metric

Data Feminism Framework

Drawing on D'Ignazio and Klein's Data Feminism (2020), this resource asks WPAs to consider power at every stage of data work. Who collected this data, and who did not? Whose goals are prioritized? Who benefits, and who does not? Collecting and analyzing data requires attention to power and to the consequences of the collection and the analysis.

05

About the Exchange

Co-directed by Kirsti Cole & Holly Hassel

The Writing Program Exchange began with a collective of writing program practitioners committed to making disciplinary expertise accessible and actionable in institutional contexts. It is a searchable, updatable resource, and not a policy document or a fixed position statement. It adapts to the range of institutions, positions, and challenges WPAs face.

The Exchange does not depend on listserv archives for searching and sorting. It is a stable web environment built to grow over time.

Disciplinary knowledge, organized for institutional action.

A note on CCCC · October 2026

On July 24, 2026, the Executive Committee of the National Council of Teachers of English decided to de-charter the Conference on College Composition and Communication. The Exchange began in a working session at the 2025 CCCC convention, and many of its resources draw on CCCC position statements. Those statements remain the field's record of its professional standards, and the Exchange continues to cite them by title and date of adoption. Archived copies are available in the CCCC-L repository (opens in a new tab). As the transition proceeds, links and citations here will be updated to show where the statements are housed.

Colleagues across the field are doing the work of this transition in different roles, and I am grateful to them. This note is mine alone. It does not speak for my co-director, the working groups, or any organization.

Kirsti Cole, Co-Director
