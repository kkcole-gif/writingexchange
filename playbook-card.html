Labor Advocacy Playbook: Reference Card

The Writing Program Exchange

01Situational Self-Assessment

Know your influence and your limits before acting.

  • What is your job security: tenure, contract, or adjunct status?
  • Who holds authority over this specific issue?
  • Which governance channels exist: senate, bargaining agreement, curriculum committee?
  • What has been tried before, and what happened?

02Identify and Connect

Map who is affected. Build a coalition before going formal.

  • Who else faces this problem directly?
  • Which informal and formal spaces exist for coalition-building?
  • Who are your administrative allies?
  • Which external pressure points exist: accreditors, unions?

03Gather Local Data

Build a fuller account with numbers and testimony.

  • Institutional research and registrar: caps, DFW rates, enrollment by modality
  • Faculty surveys: workload, capacity for feedback, time per student
  • Student surveys: quality of feedback, writing center access
  • National benchmarks: the course cap dataset and the CCCC standard

04Compare to National Standards

Cite the field's professional standards in place of personal preference.

  • CCCC: 20 students maximum per writing class, 15 preferred, 60 per instructor per term
  • National median cap in first-year composition: 22. Two-year median: 25
  • About 37 percent of 417 institutions report a cap of 20 or lower
  • Present the standard as a professional norm, not an experimental threshold

05Plan a Sharing Event

Move from private knowledge to shared understanding.

  • One page: situation, standard, gap, and one bounded request
  • Faculty workshop or retreat: build internal consensus first
  • Program review or accreditation addendum: create a formal record
  • Joint faculty letter: collective voice and shared accountability

06Move into Governance or Beyond

Use official channels first, and know the alternatives.

  • Curriculum committee, faculty senate, formal proposal
  • Trojan Horse: place the request inside an administrative priority
  • Union: grievance or contract negotiation
  • Accreditor, professional organization, or, as a last resort, the press

AContingent, non-tenure-track, or adjunct

Higher risk: protect yourself first

Build a coalition before acting. Document everything. Seek tenured allies before raising an issue in public. Consider anonymous or collective action.

BTenure-track or early-career WPA

Moderate risk: act strategically

You have formal standing to raise disciplinary issues. Cite professional standards to move the argument away from personal preference. Build faculty allies. Document administrative work as scholarship.

CTenured or experienced WPA

Lower risk: act with full authority

Lead coalition-building. Bring issues to governance formally. Use the position to protect contingent colleagues. Draw on a national profile where one exists.

When official channels are blocked

(1) Trojan Horse: place the request inside an administrative priority such as retention or accreditation. (2) Faculty senate: a non-binding resolution creates a formal record. (3) Union: consult your representative first. (4) Accreditor: frame the data in SACSCOC or HLC terms. (5) Professional organizations: confirm which consultation or statements each currently offers. (6) Press: a last resort, for severe and well-documented violations.
